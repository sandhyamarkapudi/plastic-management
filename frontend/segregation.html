Source Segregation

Waste Segregation Made Simple

When we separate waste at the very second we discard it, wet waste transforms into rich agricultural compost, and clean plastics get recycled into new products instead of rotting on roadsides.

The 4-Bin Standard

Know exactly which bin to use.

Setting up 4 small containers or separate bags in your home prevents cross-contamination.

Green Bin

Wet & Biodegradable

Kitchen scraps, fruit peels, leftover curry, eggshells, garden clippings, dry flowers.

Destination: Home or community compost pit for nutrient-rich soil.
Never put plastic bags or wrappers in here!
Blue Bin

Dry Recyclable Paper

Cardboard boxes, notebooks, newspapers, clean cartons, metal beverage cans, glass bottles.

Destination: Direct to local scrap merchants (kabadiwala) for pulping.
Keep dry and free from curries and oil!
Yellow Bin

Clean Plastic Waste

PET water bottles, milk packets, oil canisters, curd cups, plastic grocery bags, buckets.

Destination: Gram Panchayat dry waste collection or plastic shredder.
Rinse with a splash of water and let dry first.
Red Bin

Hazardous & Sanitary

Expired medicines, syringe needles, pesticide cans, sanitary napkins, diapers, old phone batteries.

Destination: Designated biomedical / hazardous municipal center.
Wrap sanitary waste securely in newspaper.

Daily Routine

The 5 Steps of Everyday Household Segregation

Follow this seamless 2-minute daily rhythm at home:

1

Collect

Place a sturdy bag or box in your utility area specifically designated for clean dry plastics.

2

Separate

Never toss oily curry leftovers, fish scraps, or tea bags together with dry wrappers or bottles.

3

Rinse

Give milk packets, pickle jars, and oil pouches a quick rinse with a splash of greywater.

4

Dry & Crush

Step on plastic bottles to flatten them. Flattened items save 75% space in your storage bin.

5

Handover

Hand over your clean dry plastics weekly to the Gram Panchayat vehicle or local scrap dealer.
