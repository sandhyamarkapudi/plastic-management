Recycling for Everyday Life

Recycling turns discard into durable resource. When plastic is kept clean and dry, it can be re-melted into new pipes, containers, and durable village roads instead of littering our rivers.

The Journey

How village plastic gets recycled.

From your kitchen counter to a brand new product in 5 clean steps:

1

Source Clean

You empty the milk pouch, oil bottle, or soda container, shake out droplets, and keep it in your dry plastic bin.

2

Collection

Weekly collection by village sanitation workers or local scrap collectors (kabadiwala) aggregates the batch.

3

Sorting at MRF

At the Material Recovery Facility, workers sort by polymer type (PET #1, HDPE #2, PP #5) and color.

4

Granulation

The sorted plastic is shredded, washed in warm alkaline bath, melted, and extruded into clean uniform pellets.

5

New Products

Pellets are molded into irrigation pipes, park benches, polyester cloth bags, or mixed into all-weather road tar.

1. Keep It Dry & Empty

Leftover liquid or cooking oil creates foul fungus inside collection sacks. Recyclers reject soggy or oily plastics because they contaminate processing vats.

2. Flatten to Save Space

Crushing plastic bottles and boxes reduces their volume by 75%. This allows village collection rickshaws to carry 4x more weight in a single fuel-efficient trip.

3. Support Local Scrap Pickers

Local scrap buyers and waste pickers are the backbone of village recycling. Giving them segregated clean plastics gives them dignity and a living wage.

Clarifications

Recycling Myths vs Facts

Myth

"All plastics melt and recycle equally."

Fact: Plastics are made of different polymers. Meltdown temperatures vary drastically. Mixing PVC with PET ruins both. Proper type separation is crucial.

Myth

"Burning plastic gets rid of it harmlessly."

Fact: Plastic never vanishes when burned; it converts into invisible toxic airborne chemicals that settle into nearby vegetables, milk, and family lungs.
