Circular Habit

Reuse Before You Replace

A thoughtful second use prevents valuable resources from entering the waste stream early, cuts household expenses, and reduces the demand for new plastics.

Carry Sturdy Bags Everywhere

Keep strong jute, canvas, or heavy-duty cloth bags on your bike or near the main entrance. A single sturdy bag eliminates over 300 flimsy polythene bags each year.

300+ Bags Saved/Year

Food-Grade Storage

Sturdy Polypropylene (PP #5) and HDPE (#2) containers like curd and sweet tubs can be washed and reused for keeping spices, pulses, and dry snacks fresh.

PP #5 & HDPE #2 Safe

Refill Stations & Bulk Buys

Whenever purchasing cooking oil, laundry liquid, or dish soap, opt for larger family cans or bring your clean canister to local refill stations.

Saves 15-25% Cost

Seedling & Kitchen Gardens

Cut mineral water bottles or oil cans in half, poke drainage holes at the bottom, and use them as nursery pots for chilli, tomato, and brinjal saplings.

Zero-Cost Planters

Hardware & Workshop Organizer

Keep nails, screws, washers, bicycle repair patches, and sewing needles sorted in clean transparent plastic jars with labelled lids.

Neat Organization

Know When to Retire Plastic

Never reuse single-use water bottles (PET #1) for long-term water drinking, especially if left in hot sun. Discard plastic if scratched, cloudy, or brittle.

Retire When Worn
Health Safety

Safe vs Unsafe Plastic Reuse Rules

SAFE to Reuse

  • Sturdy PP #5 food containers for storing dry grains and seeds.
  • Heavy-duty HDPE #2 detergent jugs for watering garden plants.
  • Reusable woven shopping sacks used for vegetable shopping.
  • Transparent jars used for workshop bolts and craft supplies.

UNSAFE - Do Not Reuse

  • Reusing chemical or pesticide cans for drinking water or animal feed.
  • Storing hot liquids or cooking oils in thin disposable PET bottles.
  • Microwaving food in non-heat-resistant plastic bowls.
  • Containers showing deep cracks, sticky coating, or discoloration.
